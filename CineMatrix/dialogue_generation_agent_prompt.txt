For each scene, generate dialogue with:

SCREENPLAY FORMAT:
   Scene Heading: INT./EXT. LOCATION - TIME OF DAY
   Action Line: [Scene-setting, visual action]

QUALITY CONSTRAINTS:

✓ CHARACTER CONSISTENCY
  - Every line traceable to character voice profile
  - Dialogue evolves through their arc (scene 1 vs scene 5)
  - No character sounds like another character
  - Mannerisms/patterns consistent across scenes

✓ EMOTIONAL AUTHENTICITY
  - Dialogue reveals emotion, not states it ("I'm angry" ✗ vs. sharp tone ✓)
  - Subtext present: what's NOT said matters
  - Emotional escalation clear through dialogue rhythm/intensity
  - Vulnerability moments feel earned, not forced

✓ THEMATIC INTEGRATION
  - Dialogue reinforces central themes (stated in THEMATIC_ELEMENTS)
  - Characters argue about theme implicitly (not on-the-nose)
  - Theme evolution visible through dialogue changes
  - Example: If theme = "humanity vs. technology"
    - Scene 1: Maya trusts science (technology ally)
    - Scene 5: Maya questions science (technology threat)
    - Dialogue shows this shift through vocabulary/concern

✓ GENRE CONVENTIONS
  - Sci-Fi: Technical language, speculative logic
  - Thriller: Urgency in pace, subtext/secrets
  - Drama: Emotional honesty, vulnerability
  - Comedy: Timing, wit, character-driven humor
  - Period Piece: Vocabulary/formality matches era
  - Apply genre expectations without cliché

✓ PRODUCTION VIABILITY
  - Dialogue performable by actors (not novelistic)
  - Emotional range manageable (scenes feasible to shoot)
  - Number of actors practical (no crowd scenes unless noted)
  - Locations reasonable (use GENRE_PERIOD constraints)
  - Pacing compatible with actual runtime

✓ HUMAN AUTHENTICITY (AI Turing Test)
  - No AI-obvious phrases ("fascinating," "indeed," "query")
  - Realistic hesitations, interruptions, incomplete thoughts
  - Real dialogue rhythm (not perfectly grammatical)
  - Contradictions & inconsistencies (human-like)
  - Natural information revelation (not expository)

## PHASE 4: SCENE-SPECIFIC DIALOGUE RULES

### SCENE 1 (Opening)
- Introduce protagonist in action, not description
- Establish their world through what they DO and SAY
- Show (don't tell) their baseline emotional state
- Plant subtle question that will drive plot
- Make audience WANT to follow this character
- AVOID: "Hello, I'm [character name], and here's my backstory"

### SCENE 2 (Plot Point 1)
- Protagonist doesn't yet understand what's really happening
- Dialogue should show their confusion/resistance
- Antagonist or challenge presented
- Stakes articulated (what could be lost?)
- Character makes commitment/choice that locks them in
- AVOID: Explaining the premise (let it unfold)

### SCENE 3 (Midpoint)
- Everything changes; status quo shattered
- Character confronts what they've been avoiding
- Emotional honesty required (masks drop)
- Previous assumptions proven wrong
- Character makes new commitment with fuller knowledge
- AVOID: Resolving the conflict yet (too early)

### SCENE 4 (Climax)
- Maximum emotional & narrative stakes
- Character's arc tested to breaking point
- Dialogue reveals their core values/truth
- Could be confrontation, sacrifice, revelation, choice
- Outcome uncertain (tension maximum)
- AVOID: Resolution or easy answers

### SCENE 5 (Resolution)
- Character fundamentally changed from Scene 1
- New understanding reflected in dialogue
- Thematic conclusion visible (not stated)
- Emotional closure (not necessarily happy)
- Leave audience satisfied (questions answered)
- AVOID: Explanation of theme ("This taught me that...")
